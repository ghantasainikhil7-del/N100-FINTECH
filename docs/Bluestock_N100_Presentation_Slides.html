Mathematical Formulas
1. Profitability & Return Ratios (Mathematical Formulations)
05 / 12

📈 Return on Equity (ROE)

Measures how efficiently management allocates shareholders' capital to generate profit.

$$\text{ROE} = \left( \frac{\text{Net Profit}}{\text{Equity Capital} + \text{Reserves}} \right) \times 100$$

Implementation Edge Case: Returns None if Total Equity ≤ 0 (avoids negative equity distortions).

⚙️ Return on Capital Employed (ROCE)

Measures total operating profitability across all providers of capital (debt + equity).

$$\text{ROCE} = \left( \frac{\text{Operating Profit} + \text{Other Income}}{\text{Total Equity} + \text{Total Borrowings}} \right) \times 100$$

Capital Employed: Standardized as Total Equity + Long/Short Term Debt.

🏢 Return on Assets (ROA)

Measures net earnings generated per rupee of total company balance sheet assets.

$$\text{ROA} = \left( \frac{\text{Net Profit}}{\text{Total Assets}} \right) \times 100$$

📊 Operating Profit Margin (OPM)

Reflects pure core operational pricing power and cost efficiency before financing and taxes.

$$\text{OPM} = \left( \frac{\text{Operating Profit}}{\text{Revenue / Net Sales}} \right) \times 100$$

2. Solvency, Leverage & Efficiency Formulations
06 / 12

⚖️ Debt-to-Equity Ratio (D/E)

Assesses financial leverage and long-term solvency risk.

$$\frac{D}{E} = \frac{\text{Total Borrowings}}{\text{Equity Capital} + \text{Reserves}}$$

Rules: Returns 0.0 if Borrowings = 0. Flags High Leverage if D/E > 5.0 (excluding Financials).

🛡️ Interest Coverage Ratio (ICR)

Evaluates the firm's cushion to service interest payments from operational profits.

$$\text{ICR} = \frac{\text{Operating Profit} + \text{Other Income}}{\text{Interest Expense}}$$

Thresholds: ICR < 1.5 flagged as 'At Risk'; Interest = 0 classified as 'Debt Free'.

💰 Net Debt Calculation

Calculates true obligations after deducting liquid assets and cash reserves.

$$\text{Net Debt} = \text{Total Borrowings} - (\text{Investments} + \text{Cash \& Equivalents})$$

🔄 Asset Turnover Ratio

Measures how efficiently corporate balance sheet assets generate top-line revenues.

$$\text{Asset Turnover} = \frac{\text{Revenue / Net Sales}}{\text{Total Assets}}$$

3. Free Cash Flow (FCF) & Valuation Multiples
07 / 12

💵 Free Cash Flow (FCF) & Conversion

Calculates discretionary cash remaining after essential capital maintenance expenditures.

$$\text{FCF} = \text{CFO} - \text{Capex} \quad \text{where} \quad \text{Capex} = \Delta \text{Fixed Assets} + \Delta \text{CWIP}$$ $$\text{FCF Conversion} = \left( \frac{\text{Free Cash Flow}}{\text{Net Profit}} \right) \times 100$$

A high conversion (>80%) indicates superior earnings quality with minimal accounting inflation.

🏷️ Valuation Multiples: P/E, P/B & EV/EBITDA

Multi-factor valuation metrics to identify growth at a reasonable price (GARP).

$$P/E = \frac{\text{Current Market Price}}{\text{EPS}} \qquad P/B = \frac{\text{Market Price}}{\text{Book Value Per Share}}$$ $$EV/\text{EBITDA} = \frac{\text{Market Cap} + \text{Debt} - \text{Cash}}{\text{Operating Profit} + \text{Other Income}}$$

📈 Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR)

$$\text{CAGR} = \left( \frac{\text{Ending Value}}{\text{Beginning Value}} \right)^{\frac{1}{n}} - 1$$

Computed across 3-year, 5-year, and 10-year horizons for Sales and Net PAT.

💸 Dividend Payout Ratio

$$\text{Dividend Payout} = \left( \frac{\text{Total Dividends}}{\text{Net Profit}} \right) \times 100 = \left( \frac{\text{DPS}}{\text{EPS}} \right) \times 100$$

4. DuPont 3-Way & Dickinson Lifecycle Models
08 / 12

🔍 DuPont 3-Way Decomposition of ROE

Deconstructs Return on Equity to determine the core driver of shareholder profitability:

$$\text{ROE} = \underbrace{\left( \frac{\text{Net Profit}}{\text{Sales}} \right)}_{\text{Net Margin}} \times \underbrace{\left( \frac{\text{Sales}}{\text{Assets}} \right)}_{\text{Asset Turnover}} \times \underbrace{\left( \frac{\text{Assets}}{\text{Equity}} \right)}_{\text{Financial Leverage}}$$
  • Margin Driven: High pricing power and moat (e.g., FMCG, IT).
  • Turnover Driven: High asset efficiency and velocity (e.g., Retail).
  • Leverage Driven: High financial risk and borrowings (e.g., NBFCs).
